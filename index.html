Cab 02 — Cone Zone

Now work
the counter.

The other side of the business. Fish the dock out the back door, churn what you catch into flavor, and build every ticket that lands on the pass — alone, at first. Make rent every night from Early Summer to the offseason, and spend what's left on machines and on people who can cover a station while you run.

Walk the shop WASD
Work the station — cast, churn, scoop J
Load a fish · dump a tub LK
  • Four steps, one room. Cast at the dock, load the fish, hold to churn, then build the order at the customer — there's no separate assembly bench. The cone fills on the counter in front of them, one press per scoop.
  • You set the board. A machine holds one flavor, and the wall board lists exactly what you're making — so people can only order what you chose to churn. Everyone has a favourite: find it on the board and they tip heavily, miss it and they settle.
  • Read the line. When the counter fills, a queue forms out the door, and everyone in it shows what they came in wanting before they reach you.
  • Learn the menu. You start knowing two recipes. The rest are studied from the Kraken's Ledger — fastest when the shop is quiet, and out at the dock when a nor'easter has shut it. Every recipe you learn is one more person you don't have to disappoint.
  • Rent comes due every night. Miss it once and the landlord takes the machines.
  • Three shops, saved as you go. Each slot writes itself down at closing time, so a season can take as many sittings as you like.
  • One machine, one flavor. Every tub you commit is a bet on what walks in next.
  • The seasons fight you. Heat wave melts your tubs, fog shortens every temper, and a nor'easter shuts the dock in squalls.
  • The bosses are customers now. The Kraken orders six, the Surfer won't wait, and the Seagull King doesn't order at all — he just takes.

The Story

Why we run.

They came in the summer of 1961. Nobody knows from where. By noon the beaches were closed, and they stayed closed, and by the end of that season the island had begun having the quiet conversations islands have when they are finished.

Then a bayman out of Beach Haven went into the water.

No two people here tell it the same way. He was gone a night, or a week, or the rest of the summer. Every version agrees on this much: he went down, he called up the Kraken, and he came back with a book.

Whatever he made out of it was enough. Flotsam and Barnacle went under, the island opened, and nobody has seen them since.

The Kraken's Ledger

Most of it cannot be read. What can does not explain itself.

cold, sweet, of the sea

they do not go. they wait.

He put it in your hands one evening and did not explain that either. He said he had to go back to the sea, and he walked off the end of the dock, and nobody on Long Beach Island has seen him since.

So it is yours now — the book, the creamery, the island. Grow the shop. Keep the traps full and the coolers cold. If they come back there will be nobody else, and you will have one job: fill their bellies, and save the town.
